A free dashboard that runs on your computer and reads real data from MetaTrader 5. It sizes positions by % risk, flags trades that break the rules you set, and shows which habits are costing you money. It only reads; it never places a trade.

Three layers that protect your money

All three share one set of rules that you set once.

  1. Before you trade

    Money and trade plan

    Enter your capital and pick a risk level. The app works out what you can lose per trade and per day, suggests a stop from the nearest swing and from ATR, shows margin and the move to broker stop-out, and simulates 2,000 runs to show the chance of losing half the account.

    Try it in the demo
  2. Right before you enter

    Pre-trade check

    Thirteen rules checked against live MT5 data: size, spread, upcoming high-impact news, today's loss, total open risk, correlated symbols, and your own history with this kind of trade.

    In the installed app
  3. After the trade closes

    Trading discipline

    Re-checks every closed trade and splits the profit and loss of rule-following and rule-breaking trades, so you see which habits cost money: jumping back in after a loss, sizing up to win it back, forgetting the stop.

    Try it in the demo

While you trade, a warning strip on every page tells you the moment you hit your daily loss limit, hold a trade without a stop, or hold trades into high-impact news.

Install in three steps

  1. Open MetaTrader 5 and log in to a demo account.
  2. Download the code from GitHub, unzip it and double-click BAT_DAU.bat. If Python is missing it asks, then installs it for you.
  3. Open the Harry Gold Research icon on your desktop.
Download from GitHub

Read-only, on your computer

The app never places, changes or closes a trade. By default it only reads demo accounts, runs on your computer and sends your account data nowhere. The source code is public on GitHub so anyone can check it.

Not investment advice. Trading CFDs and forex carries a high risk of losing money. Good risk management makes losing trades smaller; it does not make winning trades more frequent.
